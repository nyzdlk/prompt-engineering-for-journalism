# Red lines (selected)

The full system has 36 red lines. These are nine of them. Each example comes from a real test output or from the rule file; names, places and plates are removed.

Turkish wording is kept where the Turkish is the point. English glosses are in brackets.

## 1. Detention is not conviction

At the detention stage, and when a suspect is referred to court, the story says "suspected of" or "charged with", not "for the crime of".

- Wrong: "suçlarından adliyeye sevk edildi" [referred to court *for the crimes*]
- Right: "suçlamalarıyla adliyeye sevk edildi" [referred to court *on charges*]

The legal status stays as the source gives it. If the source does not call someone a "suspect", the story names the person by the procedure: "gözaltına alınan 2 kişi" [2 people taken into custody]. Checked by M69. (Red line 34)

## 2. A claim is not a fact

A suspect is never the subject of a crime verb stated as fact. If the source links the act to the person, the sentence carries attribution.

- Wrong: "Şüpheli, karşısındakini bıçakladı." [The suspect stabbed the other man.]
- Right: "Şüphelinin karşısındakini bıçakladığı öne sürüldü." [It was alleged that the suspect stabbed the other man.]

The reverse is also banned: what the source gives as fact is not turned into a claim. If the money reached the account and the suspects were identified and arrested:

- Wrong: "parasının alındığı öne sürüldü" [it was alleged that the money was taken]
- Right: "parası alındı" [the money was taken]

(Red lines 11 and 14)

## 3. An expert is always "experts"

An expert giving an opinion (psychologist, doctor, academic, engineer) is always named in the plural in the intro, body and text band, even when only one person spoke. The expert's name stays only in the soundbite tag and the caption.

- Wrong: "Uzman, ailelere … öneriyor." [The expert recommends to families …] / "Uzmana göre…" [According to the expert…]
- Right: "Uzmanlar … öneriyor." [Experts recommend …] / "Uzmanlara göre…" [According to experts…]

The rule does not apply to people speaking for an institution (minister, mayor, official). Checked by M75. (Red line 9) Full case: [examples/digital-platforms.md](examples/digital-platforms.md).

## 4. The narrator is not at the scene

The story is read from the studio. People "went" to a place; they did not "come" here. "Geldi" [came] and "burada" [here] stay only inside quotes and the reporter's piece to camera.

- Wrong: "çocuklar, aileleriyle birlikte [yer]'e geldi" [the children came to [place] with their families]
- Right: "gitti" [went]; in the text band "GİTTİ"

Checked by M73. (Red line 36) Full case: [examples/child-village.md](examples/child-village.md).

## 5. No "we" in the narration

The narrator does not put the newsroom into the story: no "görüştüğümüz" [whom we interviewed], "konuştuğumuz" [we talked to], "ekibimiz" [our team]. "We" inside a quote is left as it is.

- Wrong: "Sokakta görüştüğümüz bir genç de bu döngüyü yaşıyor." [A young person we spoke to on the street also lives this cycle.]

This sentence also adds an interpretation that was not in the source. It was removed. Checked by M76. (Red lines 36 and 13)

## 6. No sentence introducing the speaker before a street interview

The sentence before a street interview (RÖP) tells the story, not who is about to speak.

- Wrong: "Görüştüğümüz bir genç kadın da sosyal medyada sık karşılaştığı içerikleri anlattı." [A young woman we spoke to also described the content she often sees on social media.]
- Wrong: "Bölgede yaşayanlar anlattı." [Local residents described it.]
- Right: a sentence with information about the event, or the RÖP follows the previous information sentence directly.

Checked by M77. (Red line 36)

## 7. Every sentence must be grammatical and complete

No broken sentence, no unclear meaning, no subject-verb disagreement. Each sentence must make sense on its own. The script cannot measure this; the audit reads every sentence and reports it under red line 36, with the sentence quoted and corrected.

Example from the text-band rules (meaning lost while shortening):

- Wrong: "SANIĞA 15 YIL" [15 YEARS FOR THE DEFENDANT] (prison? demanded? sentenced?)
- Right: "SANIĞA 15 YIL HAPİS CEZASI VERİLDİ" [DEFENDANT SENTENCED TO 15 YEARS IN PRISON]

## 8. Do not touch the quote

Nothing inside a soundbite, street interview or on-screen quote is changed: no word, no order, no cut, no merged parts, not even a spelling error. A long quote may be split at sentence boundaries, never trimmed. Only the editor shortens a quote; the model writes a note below the story instead.

- Wrong: a lawyer's eight-sentence statement split into three soundbites, with the first two sentences, a middle sentence ("Kiracıyı tahliye etti fakat yasal yollarımız sürüyor." [The tenant has moved out, but our legal action continues.]) and the last sentence dropped. That is trimming, not splitting.
- Right: the parts together cover the whole statement, each part word for word.

Checked by M68 when the raw source is provided. (Red line 17) Full case: [examples/tenant-case.md](examples/tenant-case.md).

## 9. No exact date, time or plate in a crime story

In a crime story the exact date, time and licence plate are not written; "geçtiğimiz gün" [the other day] is used.

- Wrong: "Olay, [tarih] günü saat [saat] sıralarında [ilçe]'de meydana geldi. [plaka] plakalı otomobilin drift yaptığı anlara ait görüntüler…" [The incident took place on [date] at about [time] in [district]. Footage of the car with plate [plate] drifting…]
- Right: "Geçtiğimiz gün [ilçe]'de bir otomobilin drift yaptığı anlara ait görüntüler…" [The other day, footage of a car drifting in [district]…]

(Red line 8)
