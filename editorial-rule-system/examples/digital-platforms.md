# Case: digital platforms and violence

Voiced story about how digital platforms expose children to violent content, with one clinical psychologist as the expert. Names are removed.

Rules involved: an expert is always "experts" (uzmanlar), and no "we" in the narration.

## 1. The expert in the plural

Before (model output):

> Intro: "Klinik psikolog [Ad Soyad], algoritmaların … olumsuz etkilediğini vurguluyor." [Clinical psychologist [Name] stresses that algorithms … have a negative effect.] and "Uzman, ailelere … öneriyor." [The expert recommends to families …]
>
> Body: "[Soyad]'a göre…" [According to [Surname]…] and "Uzmana göre…" [According to the expert…]
>
> Text band: "[SOYAD]: …"

After (editor's correction):

> Intro: "Uzmanlar algoritmaların şiddet içeriklerini öne çıkararak çocukları ve gençleri olumsuz etkilediğini vurguluyor. Ailelere ise dijital okuryazarlık, algoritmayı yönlendirme ve içerik takibi öneriyor."
> [Experts stress that algorithms push violent content forward and affect children and young people negatively. To families they recommend digital literacy, steering the algorithm and monitoring content.]
>
> Body: "Uzmanlara göre çözümün ilk adımı…" [According to experts, the first step of the solution…]
>
> Text band: "UZMANLAR … ÖNERİYOR" [EXPERTS … RECOMMEND]

The expert's name stays only in the soundbite tag and the caption. The opinion of one expert is not presented as the voice of the newsroom.

## 2. "We" and the empty bridge

Wrong bridges from the same kind of story:

> "Uzman, şiddet içeriklerine sürekli maruz kalan çocukta neler değiştiğini anlattı."
> [The expert described what changes in a child constantly exposed to violent content.]

This describes the speaker and leaves the topic hanging with "neler" [what].

> "Sokakta görüştüğümüz bir genç de bu döngüyü yaşıyor."
> [A young person we spoke to on the street also lives this cycle.]

This contains "we" and an interpretation that is not in the source.
